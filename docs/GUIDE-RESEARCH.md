# Guide research and editorial decisions

Checked: 2026-11-15. Scope: seven topics for guitar, four-string bass and high-G ukulele (21 topic/instrument pairs), published in all eleven application languages.

## Search method and limits

Direct retrieval of Google’s result page was unavailable. A final attempt on 2026-11-15 returned HTTP 429; no ranking was inferred from other engines. The references below were found through web search and opened individually; they are **not represented as the first three Google results**. Ranking depends on query, country, language and date. Research was conducted in English, then the original lessons and exercises were translated into en, fr, es, de, it, pt-BR, hi, id, ja, ko and zh-CN. We did not claim 231 separate local Google searches.

For each pair, three relevant references were retained. Duplicate mirrors, unrelated product listings and inaccessible pages were replaced. Public lesson descriptions were used only for the information visible without a subscription; no paid material was accessed. The GuitarTricks barre-chord overview and the Roy Vogt course overview are limited in depth. The three guitar-triad references were replaced with fully readable lessons, and the tablature and bass-practice references were cross-checked against Fender and StudyBass. Some references are teaching PDFs rather than HTML articles. Source titles remain in their original language, with localized reference-section labels.

## Original teaching and checks

No article paragraphs, photographs, song transcriptions or source tablatures were copied. The tab sequences, count grids and exercises were written for this project. The shared musical facts were checked against instrument tunings and pitch arithmetic.

Corrections applied instead of repeating source overstatements:

- Text-only tab may omit rhythm; tab with stems, beams, rests or a rhythmic staff can specify duration.
- High-G GCEA is not ordered from lowest to highest pitch. C–E is four semitones; E–A is five.
- A new voicing is not necessarily a new inversion; the actual lowest pitch determines inversion.
- Pentatonic notes can clash with the current harmony. No universal “all notes work” promise.
- A chord-diagram × means omit the string; a tab x generally means a sounded muted note, not a rest.
- All original measures total four beats; tests check strings, frets and the advertised pitch sets.
